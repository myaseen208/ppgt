# Incidence-Matrix Diagnostics

## 1 Abstract

This vignette develops two matrix diagnostics that are useful across the
whole package:

- pool overlap through \\\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top\\
- sample co-occurrence through \\\mathbf{M}^\top\mathbf{M}\\

These diagnostics are presented as vignette material only. They are not
new exported package helpers.

They also sit outside the constructor, decoder, simulation, and workflow
APIs. The matrices \\\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top\\ and
\\\mathbf{M}^\top\mathbf{M}\\ are interpretive summaries of a fixed
design \\\mathbf{M}\\; they do not choose a decoder, execute retesting,
or implement stage-2 protocol logic.

## 2 Architecture Boundary

Within the package layer model:

1.  construction returns \\\mathbf{M}\\
2.  decoding interprets \\\mathbf{z}\\ relative to \\\mathbf{M}\\
3.  simulation generates \\\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\\ and \\\mathbf{z}\\
4.  workflow execution coordinates stage-1 and optional stage-2 logic
5.  reporting summarizes stored workflow objects

The diagnostics in this article belong to none of those execution
layers. They are read-only summaries of a fixed incidence matrix.

## 3 Pool-Overlap Matrix

For \\\mathbf{M} \in \\0,1\\^{J \times N}\\, the pool-overlap matrix is
\\\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top = \operatorname{tcrossprod}(\mathbf{M})\\.

The \\(j, j')\\ entry counts how many individuals belong to both
\\\mathcal{P}\_j\\ and \\\mathcal{P}\_{j'}\\. The diagonal entries are
the pool sizes \\n_j\\.

More explicitly, \\ (\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top)\_{jj'} = \sum\_{i=1}^N
M\_{ji} M\_{j'i} = \|\mathcal{P}\_j \cap \mathcal{P}\_{j'}\|. \\

## 4 Sample Co-Occurrence Matrix

The sample co-occurrence matrix is \\\mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{M} =
\operatorname{crossprod}(\mathbf{M})\\.

The \\(i, i')\\ entry counts how many pools contain both individuals
\\i\\ and \\i'\\. The diagonal entries are the sample coverages \\w_i\\.

Equivalently, \\ (\mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{M})\_{ii'} = \sum\_{j=1}^J
M\_{ji} M\_{ji'} = \|\mathcal{J}\_i \cap \mathcal{J}\_{i'}\|. \\

## 5 Gramian Identities And Design Interpretation

The two diagnostics can be read as exact Gramian identities: \\
\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{pool}} = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top, \qquad
\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{sample}} = \mathbf{M}^\top\mathbf{M}. \\ Their
diagonal entries recover the canonical row and column weights, \\
(\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{pool}})\_{jj} = n_j, \qquad
(\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{sample}})\_{ii} = w_i, \\ while their off-diagonal
entries recover pairwise overlaps, \\
(\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{pool}})\_{jj'} = \|\mathcal{P}\_j \cap
\mathcal{P}\_{j'}\|, \qquad (\mathbf{G}\_{\mathrm{sample}})\_{ii'} =
\|\mathcal{J}\_i \cap \mathcal{J}\_{i'}\|. \\ This viewpoint is useful
across all design families because it turns qualitative balance and
overlap questions into direct matrix identities.

## 6 Why These Diagnostics Matter

These matrices help interpret several design properties.

- Balance: the diagonals of \\\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top\\ and
  \\\mathbf{M}^\top\mathbf{M}\\ show whether \\n_j\\ and \\w_i\\ are
  constant.
- Overlap control: the off-diagonal entries show how often pools or
  individuals intersect.
- Separable and disjunct behavior: small sample co-occurrence makes it
  easier for different active sets to induce distinguishable pooled
  outcomes, and makes it easier for an outside individual to retain a
  pool that is not covered by a bounded active set.
